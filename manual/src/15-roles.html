14Role & Permission Guide

Three roles exist: the public visitor (the cook), the employee, and the mess manager. Every rule below is enforced inside the database, so it holds even if someone edits a URL, replays a form, or calls the API directly.

14.2The historical-data rule

WhoCan they change history?
EmployeeNo. A past date is refused outright. The only route is to ask a mess manager.
The month's own manager, while runningYes — any meal of their own period, any date within it, ignoring deadlines.
The month's own manager, after it endsNo. Meals become a read-only record; everything else about the month stays editable forever.
Any other month's managerNo — they cannot even read another month's data.
The one shared thing

The employee list is office-wide, not per month: any manager account — past, present or future — can add, rename, deactivate or reset a login. Agree changes before making them.

14.3What is blocked at the database, not just on screen

  • An employee writing another employee's meal row.
  • Anyone writing a meal on a past date, or today after its deadline (managers excepted).
  • A manager writing a meal outside their own running period — one forbidden meal rejects the whole write.
  • A manager reading or writing another month's data, or changing any period's dates (only the meal rate fields can be updated).
  • Deleting a login, a manager profile, a period, or an employee that still has records attached.
  • A signed-out visitor reading deposits, bills, guest bills, spending or anyone's phone number.

A padlock or a greyed-out field is only reflecting a rule the database would enforce anyway.
