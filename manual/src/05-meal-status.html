4Meal Status & Meal Selection

This section covers the single most-used feature in the application: telling the mess whether you are eating. It is deliberately tiny — three buttons — and everything interesting is in the rules around them.

4.1The three deadlines currently in force

MealDeadlineWhat happens at that time
🍳 Breakfast8:00 AMToday's breakfast button locks. It keeps showing ON or OFF, but tapping does nothing.
🍛 Lunch11:00 AMToday's lunch button locks.

These times are a setting, not a law of nature

8:00 / 11:00 / 17:00 are the values this office is running with, and they are what every screenshot in this manual shows. They are one office-wide setting that any mess manager can change at Meal Status → 🔒 Employee meal deadlines (Section 4.5). If your office has different times, the Employee Panel always prints the real ones on the line under "My meals" — trust that line, not this table.

What is different across the four? The banner and the padlocks — nothing else. The buttons always show the true ON/OFF value; the date never changes what you are counted for, only whether you may still change it. A past date gets a grey "previous days" banner, today gets an amber "deadline passed" banner listing exactly which meals are affected, and a future date gets no banner.

4.4When there is no data for a date

A date on which you have never touched anything has no record in the database at all. The panel does not show an error or an empty box — it simply shows all three meals OFF, because that is what "no record" means.

A far-off date, nothing chosen yet
Figure 4.6 — 20 October: no record exists. All three OFF, all three editable, no dish announced so no amber chips.
What is different? The buttons are identical in wording — the only difference is the padlocks, and the amber dish chips that appear in Figure 4.5 (the manager had announced a menu for that date) but not in Figure 4.6 (nothing announced). Read the padlocks, not the words.

4.5Who changes the deadlines — and how

The deadlines live on the mess manager's Meal Status tab, in a collapsed strip at the top. Any mess manager can open it; the change applies office-wide and takes effect on every employee's next page load.

4.6What happens after a deadline — exactly

  1. On screen: within 15 seconds the button grows a padlock and stops responding. No reload needed.
  2. In the database: any attempt to write that meal is rejected with MEAL_LOCKED — including an attempt made outside the application, from a script or a modified request.
  3. Your count: the meal immediately joins Meals I had and your total meal count, because it can no longer change.
  4. The cook: the plate count on the meal board is final for that meal.
  5. The fix, if you need one: ask the mess manager. A manager is not bound by the deadlines and can correct any meal of their own running period (Section 8.4).
Warning — the deadline is not a reminder, it is a wall

There is no grace period, no "request a change" button and no appeal inside the application. If you miss the lunch deadline and leave the office, you are billed for that lunch unless a mess manager removes it for you. Set your meals for the whole week in advance — future dates are always open.
